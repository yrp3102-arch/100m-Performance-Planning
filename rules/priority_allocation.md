# Priority Allocation Rules

Version: 0.1
Status: Draft
Date: 2026-09-15

## Purpose

This document defines how limited planning resources are allocated among
multiple relevant 100m performance needs.

It operationalizes priority relationships defined by the
[Plan Core Model](../core_models/01_plan_model.md), using the performance
problems and candidate bottlenecks defined by the
[100m Performance Core Model](../core_models/02_100m_performance_model.md).

It evaluates resource claims before detailed intervention selection and time
organization.

The governing relationship is:

Performance Problem

→ Candidate Bottleneck

→ Priority Allocation

→ Candidate Interventions

→ Stimulus-Dose-Cost Interpretation

→ Stress Organization

→ Planning Workflow.

Priority Allocation answers:

> What currently deserves development, retention, exploratory, or no planned
> resource allocation?

It does not identify a universally best exercise, prescribe a dose, construct
a week, or implement the feedback loop.

---

## 1. Priority Is Conditional

Priority is the current claim an object has on scarce planning resources under
a defined set of conditions.

A priority object may be a performance problem, candidate bottleneck,
determinant, supporting capacity, exposure requirement, or intervention role.

The object must be named explicitly.

Priority is conditional on:

- competitive 100m relevance;
- the athlete's current performance problem;
- the strength of the bottleneck hypothesis;
- current athlete state;
- expected marginal benefit;
- transfer potential;
- time to useful effect;
- recovery and opportunity cost;
- active constraints;
- risk;
- uncertainty;
- and competition timing.

Importance and current priority are distinct.

A quality can be important to 100m performance while receiving only
maintenance, minimal, or no current allocation because:

- it is already sufficient for the present problem;
- its marginal value is low;
- a stronger bottleneck claim exists;
- development would arrive outside the useful time window;
- its cost would compromise a higher-value exposure;
- a hard constraint excludes the relevant intervention;
- or uncertainty does not justify a large commitment.

Priority is therefore relational and time-dependent.

The system must not infer current priority from tradition, exercise reputation,
group averages, or the general importance of a capacity alone.

### 1.1 Allocation States

Every active candidate should have one explicit allocation state:

| Allocation state | Planning meaning | Resource implication |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Receives a major claim on development resources | Protect the exposures, quality, recovery, and attention needed to pursue change |
| Maintain | Receives an active retention claim | Preserve a defined ability, exposure, or tolerance range without treating further development as the main objective |
| Minimal | Receives the smallest currently justified deliberate footprint | Limit commitment while retaining optionality, information, or a plausible minimum exposure |
| Temporarily Withdrawn | Receives no planned exposure in its current role | Preserve the reason for removal and the conditions under which reconsideration becomes relevant |

These states describe allocation roles.

They are distinct from the feedback decision states defined by the
[Feedback-Decision Core Model](../core_models/04_feedback_decision_model.md)
and operationalized by the [Progression Rules](./progression.md).

For example, an intervention can remain in a Maintain allocation role while
its dose is modified, or it can be maintained as a decision while still being
Primary in the current mesocycle.

### 1.2 Priority Record

A defensible allocation should retain:

- the allocated object;
- the allocation state;
- the performance problem it serves;
- the relevant candidate bottleneck or constraint;
- the expected adaptation or preservation claim;
- the expected transfer path;
- the principal resource claims;
- the major costs and risks;
- the most important displaced alternative;
- the decision time horizon;
- supporting and conflicting evidence;
- confidence;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- and conditions that could justify reallocation.

The record supports traceability without converting allocation into a fixed
numeric algorithm.

## 2. Primary

Primary is the allocation state for an object with a leading current claim on
development resources.

A Primary allocation is justified when the available evidence supports all of
the following relationships sufficiently for the consequence of the decision:

- the object is relevant to competitive 100m performance;
- it addresses a current performance problem or limiting constraint;
- its bottleneck claim is stronger than mere weakness or association;
- a feasible intervention can plausibly influence it;
- useful adaptation and transfer can occur within the relevant time horizon;
- expected marginal benefit justifies cost, risk, and uncertainty;
- and the allocation does not violate a hard constraint.

Primary status creates protection obligations.

The plan should protect:

- access to the required conditions;
- sufficient execution quality;
- recoverability of the intended exposure;
- compatible session placement;
- decision-relevant monitoring;
- and competition expression when that is the current objective.

Supporting work should not consume these protected resources without an
explicitly superior reason.

Primary priorities must remain scarce relative to available resources.

Scarcity is not a fixed count.

It means that each additional Primary claim dilutes time, recovery, attention,
or exposure quality available to the others.

When several objects are labeled Primary, the system must be able to show that
their combined resource demand remains feasible and that none is Primary in
name only.

An object must not become Primary solely because:

- it is below a population average;
- it is easy to measure;
- it is commonly trained;
- it improved in another athlete;
- it has a plausible mechanism without athlete-specific support;
- or it has not recently appeared in the plan.

Primary status does not guarantee improvement.

It expresses the strongest current allocation claim under uncertainty.

## 3. Maintain

Maintain is an active allocation state intended to preserve a defined
capability, exposure tolerance, technical organization, or competition-relevant
quality while another object receives greater development emphasis.

A Maintain allocation requires a stated preservation target.

The target may be expressed as:

- retention within an interpretable performance range;
- continued access to a task-specific exposure;
- preservation of technical familiarity;
- preservation of tissue tolerance;
- or prevention of a strategically important decline.

Maintain does not mean ignore.

It requires:

- a represented exposure rather than a label alone;
- a plausible retention claim;
- cost compatible with the Primary priority;
- and observations capable of detecting a meaningful loss when that loss would
  change the plan.

Maintenance need is conditional.

It depends on prior adaptation, residual effects, concurrent stimuli,
individual response, time away from direct exposure, and the consequence of
loss.

No universal maintenance frequency or dose follows from the allocation label.

If the maintenance cost materially degrades Primary work, the system must
reconsider the maintenance method, dose, placement, or allocation state.

An object should not remain in Maintain merely because it has historically
been present.

Its retained value must exceed the value of the resources it consumes.

---

## 4. Minimal

Minimal is the smallest deliberate resource commitment currently justified by
the decision context.

It is appropriate when a full development or confident maintenance claim is
not justified, but zero allocation would discard meaningful optionality,
information, familiarity, or potential retention value.

Minimal can serve:

- bounded exploratory exposure;
- low-cost preservation of task familiarity;
- limited contact while tolerance is uncertain;
- retention of an intervention option;
- or a small allocation under severe resource competition.

Minimal is distinct from Maintain.

Maintain asserts a preservation objective with a plausible exposure basis.

Minimal asserts only that the selected small footprint is currently preferable
to either a larger commitment or complete withdrawal.

Minimal does not guarantee retention.

When evidence is insufficient to establish that a minimal exposure preserves
the target, the record must state that uncertainty explicitly.

Minimal must not become a rhetorical device for keeping every possible quality
inside the plan.

The cumulative cost of multiple Minimal allocations can be substantial.

Their combined time, attention, transition, tissue, and recovery demands must
therefore be evaluated as actual resource use.

---

## 5. Temporarily Withdrawn

Temporarily Withdrawn is the allocation state in which an object receives no
planned exposure in its current role for the present decision period.

Temporary withdrawal can be justified when:

- a hard constraint excludes the intervention;
- risk or tissue cost is unacceptable under current conditions;
- its marginal value is lower than the work it displaces;
- it interferes with a higher-priority objective;
- the relevant capacity is sufficiently supported by other exposures;
- the useful adaptation window has closed;
- the object no longer addresses the leading performance problem;
- or uncertainty makes the current commitment unjustified.

Temporary withdrawal is a legitimate allocation decision.

It does not establish that the target is universally unimportant, that the
method never works, or that the object can never return.

The withdrawal record should preserve:

- what has been withdrawn;
- whether the target, method, or both are withdrawn;
- why zero current allocation is preferable;
- the expected consequence of absence;
- any relevant deterioration risk;
- and the condition that would make reconsideration worthwhile.

Withdrawal should not silently delete a strategically important capability
from the planning model.

When future re-entry could matter, the system should retain a re-entry
hypothesis without precommitting to a date or dose.

## 6. Bottleneck Relevance

Priority allocation begins from a performance problem, not from an inventory
of exercises.

A candidate bottleneck has priority relevance when changing it could plausibly
change the current performance problem under the athlete's present conditions.

Weakness is not sufficient.

A variable may be low, unusual, or below a reference population without being
the current limiting factor.

The priority claim should distinguish:

- observed weakness;
- candidate bottleneck;
- limiting constraint;
- and supported causal bottleneck.

Greater bottleneck confidence can strengthen a development claim, but lower
confidence does not force either maximal commitment or complete inaction.

It may instead support a Minimal exploratory allocation when the exposure is
feasible, reversible, informative, and low enough in consequence.

When multiple bottlenecks remain plausible, allocation should preserve the
competition among explanations.

The system must not create false certainty by selecting the easiest candidate
to train or measure.

## 7. Expected Marginal Value

Allocation should be based on expected marginal value under current
conditions, not on the total historical value of a capacity or method.

Expected marginal value is the additional decision-relevant benefit expected
from allocating the next meaningful unit of scarce resource to one object
rather than to an available alternative.

It depends on:

- current level and adaptation state;
- prior and recent exposure;
- remaining room for useful change;
- responsiveness and tolerance history;
- transfer potential;
- time to benefit;
- retention needs;
- interaction with other priorities;
- and the marginal cost of further exposure.

Marginal value can decline through adaptation, redundancy, diminishing returns,
or a bottleneck shift.

It can increase when a constraint changes, a retained quality begins to decay,
a competition demand becomes more immediate, or new evidence strengthens the
bottleneck hypothesis.

Past effectiveness does not establish current Primary status.

Likewise, novelty does not establish marginal value.

When two allocations have similar expected benefit, the system may prefer the
one with lower cost, lower uncertainty, greater reversibility, clearer feedback,
or better protection of competition expression.

## 8. Transfer Potential

Priority requires an explicit relationship to competitive 100m performance.

For a supporting capacity, the intended relationship is:

Underlying Capacity

→ Sprint-Relevant Capability

→ Sprint Expression

→ Race or Segment Outcome

→ Competitive 100m Performance.

The relationship may be partial, delayed, context-dependent, masked, or absent.

An expected improvement in a supporting test does not establish a high
priority unless its transfer path addresses the current performance problem.

Transfer potential should consider:

- specificity of the expected adaptation;
- athlete-specific deficit or bottleneck relevance;
- opportunity to express the adaptation in sprinting;
- technical organization;
- fatigue and competition context;
- time available for transfer;
- and evidence from the athlete's prior response when available.

High association with elite performance does not by itself establish trainable
causation or transfer.

If transfer is the central unknown, the allocation should preserve a way to
evaluate the relevant sprint expression rather than relying only on the
supporting proxy.

## 9. Recovery and Opportunity Cost

Every allocation consumes finite resources.

Relevant resources include:

- high-quality exposure capacity;
- recovery capacity;
- local tissue tolerance;
- time;
- athlete and coach attention;
- technical bandwidth;
- monitoring capacity;
- schedule flexibility;
- and competition freshness.

Expected benefit must be evaluated together with the recovery cost required to
obtain it.

Opportunity cost must identify what the allocation displaces or degrades.

An allocation can have positive standalone benefit and still be a poor current
choice when it:

- lowers the quality of a more important exposure;
- consumes recovery needed by a stronger priority;
- removes time from a more direct transfer opportunity;
- adds complexity that reduces execution reliability;
- or reduces competition availability.

Useful does not mean necessary.

Effective does not mean the best available allocation.

The system must consider the combined cost of all allocations rather than
judging each item in isolation.

Hard constraints define the feasible resource space before priorities compete
within it, as specified by the
[Constraint Handling Rules](./constraint_handling.md).

## 10. Time Horizon and Competition Proximity

Priority depends on whether benefit can occur, transfer, stabilize, and be
expressed within the useful time horizon.

Longer-term development can justify delayed benefit when sufficient time and
recovery margin exist.

As an important competition approaches, allocation should give increasing
weight to:

- preservation of sprint-relevant qualities;
- opportunity to express existing capacity;
- reduction of avoidable residual cost;
- execution familiarity;
- and the consequence of a failed or delayed adaptation.

Competition proximity does not automatically prohibit development.

It changes the benefit-cost and uncertainty relationship.

A theoretically valuable adaptation has low current priority if it is unlikely
to become usable before the target performance window or if its acquisition
cost is likely to mask expression.

Competition itself can satisfy, partly satisfy, or disrupt an exposure need.

Its actual demand and residual cost must therefore enter allocation reasoning.

## 11. Risk and Uncertainty

Risk and uncertainty are separate allocation dimensions.

Risk concerns identifiable adverse outcomes or costs that may occur.

Uncertainty concerns how incompletely the probability, magnitude, mechanism,
response, or transfer relationship is known.

Greater uncertainty should reduce confidence in large, costly, or difficult-to-
reverse allocations when the consequence of error is high.

It does not automatically make an allocation inadmissible.

When consequence is limited and reversibility is high, uncertainty can support
a Minimal exploratory allocation whose information value may improve later
decisions.

When decision-critical uncertainty cannot be resolved within the required time,
the system may retain multiple plausible allocations or report `Cannot
Determine` rather than manufacture a unique priority.

Unknown does not mean zero benefit, zero cost, zero risk, or zero relevance.

Confidence must match the quality, applicability, and consistency of the
available evidence, following the
[Uncertainty Handling Rules](./uncertainty_handling.md).

## 12. Priority Conflicts

A priority conflict exists when two or more justified resource claims cannot
all be satisfied at their proposed level.

Conflict may arise through:

- competition for recovery;
- incompatible quality conditions;
- overlapping tissue stress;
- limited time or facility access;
- contradictory competition demands;
- excessive combined complexity;
- or displacement of a more direct 100m-relevant exposure.

The existence of several relevant targets does not remove the conflict.

The system should compare feasible allocations across:

- performance-problem relevance;
- bottleneck support;
- expected marginal benefit;
- transfer potential;
- time to benefit;
- recovery and opportunity cost;
- risk;
- uncertainty;
- reversibility;
- and competition expression.

An allocation dominated on decision-relevant dimensions should normally lose
resources unless an important unmodeled condition remains.

When no option dominates, the trade-off must remain visible.

The system may select a conditional preference, preserve more than one feasible
option, or state that available information does not identify a unique winner.

It must not hide conflict by assigning unsupported numerical weights or by
labeling every target Primary.

Detailed comparison follows the [Plan Comparison Rules](./plan_comparison.md).

## 13. Priority Reallocation

Priority is provisional and must be reconsidered when decision-relevant
conditions materially change.

Reallocation can be supported by changes in:

- the observed performance problem;
- the candidate bottleneck hypothesis;
- actual exposure or achieved quality;
- expected versus observed response;
- adaptation or retention trend;
- realized transfer;
- fatigue, recovery, or tissue state;
- exposure tolerance;
- cost or interference;
- hard constraints;
- available alternatives;
- competition timing;
- or uncertainty and confidence.

Reallocation should match the scope and strength of the new evidence.

A local noisy observation does not usually justify rewriting the whole priority
structure.

A high-consequence constraint change can justify immediate removal or broader
reallocation without waiting for a repeated trend.

Reallocation may change:

- the allocation state;
- the object holding the state;
- the protected resource;
- the intervention used to serve the object;
- or the balance among competing claims.

Changing the method does not necessarily change the target priority.

Changing the target priority does not by itself specify the replacement method
or dose.

The feedback basis, confidence, and decision scope are defined by the
[Feedback-Decision Core Model](../core_models/04_feedback_decision_model.md).

Operational progression and transition conditions remain in the
[Progression Rules](./progression.md) and
[Stage Transition Rules](./stage_transition.md).

## 14. Priority Allocation Invariants

The planning system must preserve the following rules:

1. Competitive 100m performance remains the terminal criterion for priority
   claims.
2. General importance does not establish current priority.
3. A measured weakness does not automatically establish a bottleneck or a
   Primary allocation.
4. Priority is conditional on athlete state, time, constraints, cost, risk, and
   uncertainty.
5. Primary status must remain scarce relative to the resources required to
   protect it; scarcity does not imply a universal count.
6. Maintain is an active preservation allocation with a defined object and
   purpose.
7. Minimal is a bounded commitment and does not guarantee maintenance.
8. Temporarily Withdrawn preserves the rationale and possible re-entry
   relationship rather than declaring permanent irrelevance.
9. Expected marginal value matters more than historical reputation or method
   novelty.
10. Supporting adaptation requires a plausible transfer relationship to the
    current 100m performance problem.
11. Recovery cost and opportunity cost must be evaluated explicitly.
12. The cost of several small allocations must be considered in combination.
13. Hard constraints define the feasible allocation space before feasible
    priorities are compared.
14. Competition timing changes allocation value because development and
    expression have different time and cost relationships.
15. Risk and uncertainty remain distinct and visible.
16. When no allocation clearly dominates, the remaining trade-off must not be
    hidden by false precision.
17. Priority may change when athlete state, response, constraints, or
    competition context changes.
18. A reallocation must be proportional to the evidence and must preserve the
    distinction between target, intervention, dose, and time organization.

## 15. Rule Interfaces

[Conditional Appropriateness Rules](./conditional_appropriateness.md) determine
whether an otherwise valid allocation fits the athlete and current conditions.

[Constraint Handling Rules](./constraint_handling.md) define the feasible space
within which resource claims can compete.

[Plan Comparison Rules](./plan_comparison.md) compare feasible alternatives
when allocations involve unresolved trade-offs.

[Stimulus-Dose-Cost Core Model](../core_models/03_stimulus_dose_cost_model.md)
represents candidate interventions after a target receives an allocation claim.

[Stress Organization Rules](./stress_organization.md) determine how selected
stimuli can coexist in time without silently violating the allocation.

[Feedback-Decision Core Model](../core_models/04_feedback_decision_model.md)
supplies the evidence-weighted state update that can justify later
reallocation.

Domain files may supply candidate methods and task-specific characteristics.

Workflows may order allocation, comparison, planning, and review.

This Rule does not provide an exercise library, assign universal doses, build a
weekly schedule, or prescribe the order of a planning audit.
