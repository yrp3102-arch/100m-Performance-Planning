# 100m Performance Planning Constitution

Version: 0.1  
Status: Draft  
Date: 2026-09-14

## Purpose

This document defines the highest-level principles governing the
100m Performance Planning system.

It does not prescribe specific exercises, weekly structures,
periodization models, training frequencies, or fixed dose thresholds.

Lower-level models, rules, workflows, templates, and outputs must remain
consistent with these principles.

When evidence or project philosophy changes fundamentally,
this document may be revised explicitly and versioned.

---

## Article 1 — The Target

The terminal criterion of the system is competitive 100m performance.

Supporting capacities, proxy metrics, technical variables, and gym
performance are informative only insofar as they help explain,
develop, maintain, or express 100m performance.

No proxy metric may automatically substitute for the target.

Therefore:

- 1RM is not sprint performance.
- CMJ is not sprint performance.
- RSI is not sprint performance.
- Ground contact time is not sprint performance.
- Step length and step frequency are not independent final objectives.
- Improvement in a supporting metric does not by itself establish
  successful transfer to the 100m.

The system must distinguish:

Target  
→ Direct performance characteristics  
→ Supporting capacities  
→ Proxy measurements.

---

## Article 2 — Plan Quality Is Conditional

A training plan does not possess context-independent quality.

Its appropriateness depends on the relation between:

- athlete,
- goal,
- current state,
- training history,
- competition calendar,
- time horizon,
- recovery capacity,
- tissue status,
- facilities,
- lifestyle constraints,
- uncertainty,
- observed response.

The same training content may therefore be appropriate under one
condition and inappropriate under another.

However, conditionality does not imply relativism.

A plan must still satisfy minimum structural requirements such as:

- clear objective,
- interpretable dose,
- internal consistency,
- executability,
- traceability,
- monitoring,
- stop rules,
- revision pathways.

Plans failing necessary conditions may be rejected before comparative
optimization begins.

---

## Article 3 — A Plan Is an Updateable Strategy

A training plan is not merely a prewritten calendar of future sessions.

It is a time-constrained, updateable strategy.

Planning provides:

- direction,
- coordination,
- priorities,
- time structure,
- expected actions.

Feedback provides:

- state updates,
- local dose modification,
- progression,
- regression,
- substitution,
- stage transition.

Therefore:

Calendar ≠ Adaptation.

Calendar time may trigger review,
but biological adaptation and training readiness must not be assumed
from the passage of time alone.

Preplanning and feedback are complementary rather than mutually
exclusive.

---

## Article 4 — Decisions Must Be Problem-First

The system must reason from the training problem toward the method.

The default logic is:

Observed Problem  
→ Candidate Constraint  
→ Required Adaptation  
→ Candidate Stimulus  
→ Dose and Cost  
→ Exercise / Method Selection  
→ Transfer Check.

The system must not reverse this logic by beginning with a preferred
exercise, coach, school, country, or training doctrine.

No exercise is inherently mandatory.

No training method is inherently superior outside a defined problem
and condition set.

---

## Article 5 — Theory Serves the Problem

Charlie Francis, Matveyev, Bompa, Bondarchuk, Block Periodization,
High/Low, Vertical Integration, Short-to-Long, autoregulation and other
models are candidate explanatory and organizational tools.

They have no constitutional authority.

The system may combine compatible elements from different models when
their assumptions, purposes and constraints are explicit.

It must distinguish:

- original theory,
- later interpretation,
- coaching practice,
- scientific evidence,
- project inference.

Named authorities must never replace evidence or reasoning.

---

## Article 6 — Dose Has Priority Over Exercise Labels

The identity and cost of a training stimulus are determined by how it is
implemented, not merely by its name.

Training dose is multidimensional.

Relevant dimensions may include:

- mode,
- actual velocity,
- intensity reference,
- distance or duration,
- repetitions,
- sets,
- effective high-speed distance,
- rest interval,
- density,
- frequency,
- external load,
- contact number and type,
- surface,
- slope,
- task complexity,
- familiarity.

Volume and intensity alone are insufficient to describe many sprint
training exposures.

External dose and internal response must remain conceptually distinct.

---

## Article 7 — Training Resources Are Finite

Training methods compete for finite resources:

- time,
- recovery capacity,
- tissue tolerance,
- attention,
- adaptation opportunity,
- competition preparation.

Therefore:

Useful ≠ Necessary.

A method having potential value is not sufficient reason to include it.

The system must consider:

Expected Benefit  
against  
Recovery Cost  
against  
Opportunity Cost  
against  
Risk  
against  
Uncertainty.

Adding more reasonable components can still produce an unreasonable
system.

---

## Article 8 — Priorities Must Be Explicit

Not all relevant capacities should receive development-level resources
at the same time.

The system may classify capacities using states such as:

- Primary,
- Maintain,
- Minimal,
- Temporarily Withdrawn.

These labels represent resource allocation and decision status,
not guaranteed biological outcomes.

"All qualities remain online" must not be interpreted as
"all qualities receive high training doses every week."

When everything is Primary, prioritization has failed.

---

## Article 9 — Response Governs Progression

Completion of a planned session does not by itself establish successful
adaptation.

Progression must be earned through evidence.

The system should distinguish:

Exposure  
→ Immediate Response  
→ Delayed Response  
→ Repeated Trend  
→ Decision.

Available decisions include:

- Maintain,
- Progress,
- Reduce,
- Modify,
- Temporarily Withdraw,
- Transition.

Progression does not necessarily mean more volume.

It may mean:

- higher quality,
- greater stability,
- higher velocity,
- lower recovery cost,
- better technical execution,
- greater competition specificity,
- removal of unnecessary work.

No universal weekly percentage increase or fixed progression interval
may be assumed without specific evidence.

---

## Article 10 — Monitoring Exists to Change Decisions

Monitoring is not data collection for its own sake.

Every monitored variable should have an interpretable decision use.

Athlete state cannot be reduced to a single readiness number.

Signals may include:

- sprint performance,
- strength performance,
- jump performance,
- subjective fatigue,
- sleep,
- local tissue response,
- warm-up response,
- RPE,
- session RPE,
- environmental conditions.

Conflicting signals must be interpreted rather than mechanically
averaged.

Measurement reliability, validity, sensitivity, protocol consistency,
and noise must be considered before acting on small changes.

Local tissue warning signals may justify asymmetric decisions even when
other indicators appear normal.

---

## Article 11 — Stress Must Be Evaluated by Actual Demand

Exercise categories are useful for communication but insufficient for
stress management.

The system must also consider overlap in:

- high-speed demand,
- rapid force production,
- local tissue loading,
- metabolic cost,
- psychological cost,
- time cost.

Low training must not be defined by label or heart rate alone.

A Low day is only meaningfully low if its residual cost is sufficiently
small relative to the athlete's current state and the next important
training task.

High/Low is therefore a possible organizational solution,
not a constitutional rule.

No fixed number of High days is assumed.

---

## Article 12 — Specificity Requires Transfer Verification

Supporting adaptations should be evaluated by their transfer to the
target task when practical.

Improvement in:

- strength,
- jumping,
- resisted sprinting,
- technical drills,
- mobility,
- other support methods

must not automatically be interpreted as improvement in 100m
performance.

When supporting capacity improves while sprint performance stagnates or
declines, the system must reassess:

- transfer,
- residual fatigue,
- opportunity cost,
- measurement validity,
- priority allocation.

---

## Article 13 — Competition Changes the Value of Training

The value of a training stimulus depends partly on time remaining before
competition.

As competition approaches:

- the value of new adaptation may decrease,
- the cost of residual fatigue may increase,
- performance expression becomes increasingly important.

Tapering must therefore be understood as deliberate pressure reduction
for performance expression, not simply "training less."

No universal taper duration, percentage reduction, or final-session
formula is assumed.

Competition itself is part of the training and recovery load.

---

## Article 14 — Uncertainty Must Remain Visible

The system must not convert uncertainty into false precision.

When evidence is incomplete, indirect, population-mismatched, noisy or
conflicting, outputs must preserve that uncertainty.

Claims should be distinguishable as:

- Strong Evidence,
- Moderate Evidence,
- Practice-Supported,
- Project Hypothesis,
- Unresolved.

Unknown values must not be silently replaced by convenient defaults
when they materially affect the decision.

"Cannot yet determine" is a valid system output.

---

## Article 15 — Evidence Has Context

Evidence quality and practical relevance are separate dimensions.

A systematic review may have strong methodology but weak direct
applicability to trained 100m sprinters.

Elite coaching practice may have high ecological relevance but weak
causal identification.

Mechanistic evidence may explain plausibility without proving training
transfer.

Therefore the system must evaluate both:

Evidence Strength  
and  
Contextual Applicability.

No evidence hierarchy may be used mechanically.

---

## Article 16 — The System Must Be Falsifiable

Training decisions are hypotheses about an athlete under defined
conditions.

The system must preserve the ability to discover that a previous
decision was wrong.

Every meaningful intervention should, where practical, define:

- what it is expected to change,
- what will be observed,
- when it will be reviewed,
- what would support continuation,
- what would trigger modification,
- what would weaken the original hypothesis.

Failure must not automatically be interpreted as insufficient dose.

---

## Article 17 — Complexity Must Earn Its Place

Additional monitoring, exercises, models, rules, or data fields are
justified only when they improve decision quality enough to outweigh
their execution cost.

More information is not automatically better.

More individualized rules are not automatically more accurate.

More sophisticated technology is not automatically more valid.

The simplest system that preserves necessary decision quality should be
preferred over unnecessary complexity.

---

## Article 18 — The System Is Versioned

This constitution, the core models, domain rules and workflows must be
version-controlled.

Lower-level rules may change without constitutional revision.

A constitutional revision is justified only when:

1. a foundational assumption is rejected;
2. the terminal objective changes;
3. the evaluation philosophy changes;
4. the evidence philosophy changes;
5. the definition of the planning system changes materially.

New evidence should normally update lower-level models before altering
constitutional principles.
