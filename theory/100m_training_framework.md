# 100m Training Framework

Version: 0.1
Status: Project Default / Conditional / Revisable
Date: 2026-09-15

## Purpose

This document defines the project's first default training framework for
competitive 100m performance planning.

It provides a preferred starting prior when current Athlete State, constraints,
evidence, and response do not justify another model.

It is not a Universal Sprint Law.

Every claim is conditional, falsifiable, versioned, and subordinate to the
[Constitution](../CONSTITUTION.md).

The framework must be interpreted through:

- [100m Performance Core Model](../core_models/02_100m_performance_model.md);
- [Stimulus-Dose-Cost Core Model](../core_models/03_stimulus_dose_cost_model.md);
- [Feedback-Decision Core Model](../core_models/04_feedback_decision_model.md);
- [Priority Allocation Rules](../rules/priority_allocation.md);
- [Stress Organization Rules](../rules/stress_organization.md);
- and current Evidence and Athlete State.

Theory provides prior structure.

Evidence updates confidence.

Actual Exposure and athlete response update confidence again.

No project preference bypasses feasibility, conditional appropriateness,
transfer reasoning, or uncertainty.

## 1. High-Quality Sprint Output Has Priority

**Claim — TF-01**
Training value for competitive 100m performance depends strongly on the
athlete's ability to produce high-quality neural-mechanical organization under
sprint-specific time, velocity, and force constraints.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Evidence-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Competitive sprinting requires coordinated force application, rapid output,
technical organization, and task-specific expression at high velocity.

Training that never produces the required exposure cannot be assumed to develop
or preserve that expression.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Protect Athlete State, environment, spacing, execution quality, and recovery
conditions needed for the current high-value sprint exposure.

Interpret Actual Exposure and Achieved Quality rather than counting a sprint
label as proof of stimulus.

**Applicable Conditions**
The claim applies when high-quality sprint output is relevant to the current
Performance Problem, priority, and feasible exposure space.

**Boundary / Limitation**
`Neural-mechanical` describes an interacting task demand.

It does not imply that the nervous system determines everything, that all
fatigue is central, or that one neural-load score explains sprint training.

**Evidence Status**
The performance relevance of velocity, mechanics, and exposure quality is
supported; exact dose, spacing, and athlete-specific response remain
conditional.

**Revision Trigger**
Repeated high-quality evidence or athlete response showing that the protected
exposure does not serve the current problem, or that another feasible exposure
has greater marginal value, should reduce its priority.

## 2. Sprint-First Principle

**Claim — TF-02**
When the objective is competitive 100m improvement, direct sprint exposure is
the default highest-specificity training category.

Sprint-First ≠ Sprint-Only.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Evidence-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Direct sprinting most closely contains the competition's time, velocity,
coordination, and force-application constraints.

Supporting training can influence relevant capacities but requires a transfer
path back to sprint expression.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Allocate and protect sufficient direct sprint exposure when feasible.

Use strength, power, plyometric, conditioning, and drill work to support a
defined sprint-relevant capability rather than replace sprinting by default.

**Applicable Conditions**
The principle applies when direct sprinting is feasible, safe, and appropriate
for the athlete's state and current objective.

**Boundary / Limitation**
Medical, tissue, facility, development, or competition constraints may reduce,
modify, or temporarily remove direct exposure.

Supporting training may receive Primary allocation when it addresses the
strongest current bottleneck under those conditions.

**Evidence Status**
Specificity and transfer logic are supported; the optimal balance between
direct and supporting work remains athlete- and context-dependent.

**Revision Trigger**
Evidence that direct exposure is currently infeasible, poorly tolerated, or
lower in marginal value than a supporting intervention should modify the
allocation without discarding the terminal sprint criterion.

## 3. Quality-Dominant Sprint Training

**Claim — TF-03**
Core speed-oriented sprint work should be organized around exposure quality,
not exhaustion or prescribed-volume completion.

Fatigue ≠ Training Quality.

More Exhaustion ≠ Better Sprint Adaptation.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Evidence-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Velocity, coordination, timing, force application, and technical organization
can change as fatigue accumulates.

A task that loses the defining qualities of the Intended Stimulus may become a
different exposure.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Define what counts as useful exposure, set task-specific quality and stop
conditions, provide adequate recovery, and record Planned versus Actual
Exposure.

**Applicable Conditions**
This priority is strongest when the target is acceleration quality,
maximum-velocity expression, technical stability, or another quality-sensitive
output.

**Boundary / Limitation**
Some speed-endurance or race-model exposures intentionally include fatigue.

Their acceptable performance-cost relationship must be defined in advance;
unplanned collapse cannot be relabeled as successful training.

**Evidence Status**
Quality loss and nonlinear dose-cost relationships are supported; universal
quality thresholds are not.

**Revision Trigger**
Athlete-calibrated evidence that a different quality-cost relationship produces
better relevant adaptation without unacceptable cost should revise the local
prescription or claim scope.

## 4. Short-to-Long as a Preferred Heuristic

**Claim — TF-04**
Shorter sprint exposures progressing toward longer race-relevant exposures are
a preferred default development heuristic.

Short-to-Long is not a universal fixed sequence.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Practice-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Shorter exposures can permit controlled development of acceleration, task
quality, and exposure tolerance before larger spatial and fatigue demands are
emphasized.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Consider an emphasis trajectory such as shorter acceleration toward extended
acceleration, transition or maximum-velocity exposure, and longer race-relevant
or speed-endurance exposure.

Define the trajectory through state change and exit criteria rather than fixed
calendar dates.

**Applicable Conditions**
The heuristic is most relevant when the Athlete State, training horizon,
technical stability, and exposure history support gradual extension.

**Boundary / Limitation**
Maximum velocity, longer exposure, or competition work may have higher current
marginal value and can be introduced earlier or emphasized differently.

The heuristic does not require every athlete to pass through identical stages.

**Evidence Status**
Practice-Supported with indirect conceptual support; comparative evidence for
one universal sequencing model remains limited and contested.

**Revision Trigger**
Repeated response, competition timing, bottleneck evidence, or stronger
comparative evidence showing lower value from the default sequence should
justify deviation or status revision.

## 5. Short-to-Long With Vertical Integration

**Claim — TF-05**
The preferred short-to-long direction should usually evolve Primary emphasis
while other relevant qualities remain available at conditionally justified
levels.

All Qualities Online ≠ All Qualities High Dose.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Practice-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Abruptly removing all contact with a relevant quality can reduce familiarity,
retention, exposure tolerance, or later transition quality.

Maintaining every quality at development dose would exceed finite resources.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Use Primary, Maintain, Minimal, and Temporarily Withdrawn allocation states to
represent changing emphasis.

Retain a quality only when its current benefit exceeds recovery, opportunity,
tissue, attention, and complexity cost.

**Applicable Conditions**
Integration is useful when retained exposure supports future progression,
current competition expression, or preservation at acceptable cost.

**Boundary / Limitation**
No quality or method must remain present every Week.

Temporarily Withdrawn is legitimate when cost, risk, constraints, or low
marginal value justify absence.

Minimal exposure does not guarantee maintenance.

**Evidence Status**
Practice-Supported and conceptually consistent with retention and priority
models; exact minimum exposures are often unresolved in trained sprinters.

**Revision Trigger**
Evidence that retained qualities add cost without meaningful retention,
transfer, readiness, or re-entry benefit should reduce or remove them.

## 6. Vertical Integration

**Claim — TF-06**
Multiple relevant performance qualities may remain represented across a Cycle
at different allocation levels rather than appearing only in mutually exclusive
phases.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Practice-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Performance determinants interact, adaptations have different residual effects,
and competition can require simultaneous expression of several qualities.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Define the current adaptation problem first, then assign each relevant quality
an allocation state and exposure role.

Treat traditional phase labels as candidate organizational descriptions rather
than constitutional sequence rules.

**Applicable Conditions**
Concurrent representation is justified when exposures can coexist without
unacceptable interference, recovery demand, or loss of Primary quality.

**Boundary / Limitation**
Vertical integration does not require permanent exposure, equal priority, or
all-qualities-high-dose programming.

Hard constraints and competition preparation can narrow the represented set.

**Evidence Status**
Practice-Supported; exact organization and superiority over alternative models
remain context-dependent.

**Revision Trigger**
Repeated interference, poor execution reliability, excessive cumulative cost,
or evidence favoring a narrower temporary focus should alter the integration
pattern.

## 7. Neural-Mechanical Quality Before Low-Value Fatigue

**Claim — TF-07**
Low-value fatigue should not routinely compromise higher-value sprint-specific
velocity, rapid force expression, coordination, or technical quality.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Evidence-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Metabolic disturbance, soreness, session exhaustion, and residual fatigue can
reduce the quality or availability of later priority exposures.

These fatigue signals are costs, not direct proof of beneficial adaptation.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Sequence and distribute work to protect Primary quality, and remove or modify
supporting work whose marginal benefit does not justify interference.

**Applicable Conditions**
The claim applies when the protected exposure has greater current 100m-relevant
marginal value than the work creating the avoidable fatigue.

**Boundary / Limitation**
Speed endurance, metabolic contribution, and general conditioning can have
value when they address the current problem at acceptable cost.

The claim does not reduce all fatigue to a nervous-system mechanism or prohibit
all demanding supporting training.

**Evidence Status**
Supported at the level of fatigue, interference, and opportunity-cost logic;
the exact mechanism and schedule remain task- and athlete-dependent.

**Revision Trigger**
Evidence that the supposedly low-value fatigue produces greater relevant
benefit, or that the protected work has low current value, should revise the
comparison.

## 8. High / Low as an Organizational Heuristic

**Claim — TF-08**
High / Low can be used as a stress-clustering strategy to concentrate demanding
exposures and preserve genuine recovery opportunities.

It is not a fixed weekly template.

**Project Status**
Practice-Supported / Conditional / Contested.

**Rationale**
Clustering compatible demands may protect lower-demand periods, reduce
transition cost, and clarify recovery structure.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Classify actual multidimensional demand rather than exercise labels, then
compare clustering with separation through the
[Stress Organization Rules](../rules/stress_organization.md).

**Applicable Conditions**
High / Low is appropriate when clusters remain tolerable, later work retains
required quality, local tissue overlap is acceptable, and nominal Low periods
actually preserve recovery.

**Boundary / Limitation**
No fixed number of High days, universal same-day order, heart-rate definition
of Low, or generic CNS explanation follows from this heuristic.

Separation may be preferable for athletes with low session tolerance, schedule
constraints, incompatible quality needs, or concentrated tissue risk.

**Evidence Status**
Practice-Supported with indirect evidence; universal superiority and precise
organization are unresolved and may be contested.

**Revision Trigger**
Repeated loss of Primary quality, hidden Low-day cost, excessive local stress,
or better outcomes from another feasible organization should replace the local
High / Low solution.

## 9. Sprint-Specific Transfer Has Priority

**Claim — TF-09**
Supporting adaptation has 100m value only through a plausible and eventually
observable transfer relationship to sprint capability, expression, or race
performance.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Evidence-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Strength, power, jump, drill, and mechanical outcomes occupy different model
layers from competitive 100m performance.

Improvement in a supporting object does not guarantee change in sprint
expression.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Represent:

Underlying Capacity

→ Sprint-Relevant Capability

→ Sprint Expression

→ Race Performance.

Monitor the closest decision-relevant transfer link rather than optimizing only
the easiest proxy.

**Applicable Conditions**
Transfer evaluation must consider current bottleneck, specificity, Athlete
State, technical organization, dose, fatigue, time, and competition context.

**Boundary / Limitation**
Transfer may be partial, delayed, masked, redundant, or absent.

A race improvement also does not prove that the intended supporting adaptation
caused it.

**Evidence Status**
The distinction between proxy, capacity, and performance is strongly supported
as a reasoning boundary; specific transfer estimates remain uncertain.

**Revision Trigger**
Repeated supporting improvement without the expected sprint-relevant change
should reduce transfer confidence and intervention priority.

## 10. Strength and Power Are Supporting Systems

**Claim — TF-10**
Strength, power, and stretch-shortening-cycle qualities can be important
supporting systems, but their planning value is conditional on the current 100m
problem and transfer relationship.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Evidence-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
These capacities may influence force production, rapid output, tissue capacity,
or trainability while remaining distinct from sprint performance.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Evaluate supporting work by bottleneck relevance, expected adaptation,
transfer, opportunity cost, recovery cost, tissue stress, technical effect, and
competition proximity.

Use the simplest feasible tool that produces the required exposure with an
acceptable cost profile.

**Applicable Conditions**
Supporting work deserves greater allocation when the relevant capacity is a
plausible bottleneck and direct or alternative exposure cannot address it more
efficiently.

**Boundary / Limitation**
More strength does not automatically mean greater speed.

Higher power, CMJ, RSI, bar velocity, or 1RM does not automatically establish
100m improvement.

No single lift or jump is universally required.

**Evidence Status**
Evidence supports possible benefits and population-level relationships, with
variable specificity and athlete-level transfer.

**Revision Trigger**
Low transfer, excessive cost, redundant adaptation, or a changed bottleneck
should move supporting work toward Maintain, Minimal, or Temporarily Withdrawn.

## 11. Priority Over Completeness

**Claim — TF-11**
The plan should allocate finite resources toward the highest current expected
marginal value rather than attempting to maximize every quality.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Project Hypothesis / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Time, recovery, tissue tolerance, attention, high-quality exposure capacity,
and competition freshness are finite.

Every inclusion displaces or degrades an alternative.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Use Primary, Maintain, Minimal, and Temporarily Withdrawn to make resource
allocation explicit.

Evaluate combined cost and opportunity cost, including the accumulation of many
small elements.

**Applicable Conditions**
The claim applies whenever relevant training demands compete for limited
resources, which is expected in most real plans.

**Boundary / Limitation**
Priority does not justify ignoring important maintenance, safety, development,
or competition requirements.

Primary scarcity has no universal fixed count.

**Evidence Status**
Project Hypothesis grounded in resource and marginal-value logic; exact optimal
allocation cannot be specified universally.

**Revision Trigger**
Evidence that broader allocation improves performance or robustness without
meaningful opportunity cost should revise the chosen priority structure.

## 12. Exposure Before Capacity-Deficit Assumption

**Claim — TF-12**
When a performance quality lacks recent valid exposure, poor expression should
not be attributed prematurely to an underlying capacity deficit.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Evidence-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Poor performance can reflect insufficient exposure, low Achieved Quality,
fatigue, technical instability, measurement error, environment, tissue state,
or true capacity limitation.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Reconstruct recent Actual Exposure before selecting additional supporting work.

When safe and feasible, a bounded high-quality exposure may provide information
about expression, tolerance, and the bottleneck hypothesis.

**Applicable Conditions**
The claim applies when exposure history or fidelity is missing, changed, or
insufficient to interpret the observed result.

**Boundary / Limitation**
Exposure is not always safe or sufficient to distinguish causes.

Medical, tissue, and safety constraints can require conservative action or
external assessment.

**Evidence Status**
Supported by the prescription-exposure and observation-interpretation
distinctions; the correct exploratory exposure remains context-dependent.

**Revision Trigger**
Repeated valid exposure with persistent limitation and adequate recovery should
increase confidence in a capacity, technical, or other bottleneck explanation.

## 13. Adaptation Must Be Response-Governed

**Claim — TF-13**
Planning direction can be prospective, but progression and transition must be
constrained by Actual Exposure, Observed Response, trend, constraints, and
competition timing.

Calendar Progression ≠ Biological Progression.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Evidence-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Prescription does not guarantee stimulus, adaptation, tolerance, or transfer.

Athlete State is partially observable and changes through training and context.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Define Expected Response, quality criteria, stop conditions, review points, and
exit conditions before execution.

Use the [Review Training Workflow](../workflows/review_training.md) and existing
Progression and Stage Transition Rules to update the smallest justified level.

**Applicable Conditions**
All active training decisions require response awareness, with evidence demands
scaled to consequence and reversibility.

**Boundary / Limitation**
Response-governed does not mean random daily change, constant testing, or
overreaction to noise.

Stability is a valid decision when evidence does not justify change.

**Evidence Status**
Supported as an adaptive-planning principle; universal thresholds and response
windows are not supported.

**Revision Trigger**
If the chosen monitoring cannot change decisions, or repeated review creates
instability without better outcomes, simplify the feedback structure.

## 14. Competition Is Both Outcome and Exposure

**Claim — TF-14**
Competition is simultaneously terminal-performance evidence, a high-specificity
Actual Exposure, a stress event, and a feedback source.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Evidence-Supported / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Races contain sprint, warm-up, technical, psychological, environmental,
logistical, tissue, and recovery demands while also providing the most direct
performance outcome.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Integrate competition into exposure, stress, freshness, priority, and review
logic.

Account for what the competition replaces, satisfies, disrupts, or reveals.

**Applicable Conditions**
The specific role depends on competition importance, rounds, timing, travel,
environment, and subsequent training or racing.

**Boundary / Limitation**
A race is not a free addition to a normal Week.

One result does not classify the complete plan as good or bad, nor prove every
underlying adaptation.

Decision quality remains distinct from outcome.

**Evidence Status**
Supported as an exposure and planning relationship; precise competition-load
effects remain event- and athlete-specific.

**Revision Trigger**
Actual competition exposure and recovery that differ from the planning
assumption should update nearby organization, state, and future competition
integration.

## 15. Simplicity Has Value When Sufficient

**Claim — TF-15**
When feasible options have similar expected 100m-relevant value, prefer the
least complex option that preserves necessary decision quality.

**Project Status**
Project Default / Project Hypothesis / Conditional.

**Rationale**
Lower complexity can improve execution reliability, interpretability, athlete
understanding, monitoring burden, traceability, and recovery allocation.

**Expected Planning Implication**
Remove elements, distinctions, tests, or methods that do not materially change
stimulus, decision quality, risk handling, or transfer assessment.

Prefer a simpler candidate when benefit, cost, risk, and uncertainty are
otherwise similar.

**Applicable Conditions**
The claim applies only after necessary structure, constraints, and meaningful
differences among options are represented.

**Boundary / Limitation**
Simplicity is not the terminal objective.

Do not remove information or structure needed for safety, exposure quality,
adaptation, uncertainty, or decision update.

**Evidence Status**
Project Hypothesis supported by execution and information-cost logic; the best
level of complexity remains context-dependent.

**Revision Trigger**
If simplification removes a meaningful distinction, reduces performance value,
or prevents correct feedback, restore the necessary structure.

## Framework Interaction

The fifteen claims are not independent commandments.

They interact through the current planning problem.

The default relationship is:

Competitive 100m Objective

→ Athlete State and Performance Problem

→ Candidate Bottleneck

→ Sprint-First and Transfer Reasoning

→ Priority Allocation

→ Candidate Intervention and Dose-Cost Interpretation

→ Quality and Stress Organization

→ Actual Exposure and Response

→ Framework Confidence Update.

Short-to-Long and Vertical Integration propose a broad emphasis structure.

Priority Allocation determines what receives resources now.

High / Low proposes one possible stress arrangement.

Response-governed adaptation determines whether the current structure should
continue.

No heuristic overrides a hard constraint or the terminal performance criterion.

## Excluded Interpretations

This framework rejects the following as default reasoning:

- CNS mysticism;
- universal nervous-system fatigue explanations;
- a fixed High / Low calendar;
- a fixed Short-to-Long progression;
- fixed weekly sprint volumes;
- universal repetition or set prescriptions;
- one ideal sprint technique;
- one universal elite-athlete model;
- all-qualities-high-dose programming;
- strength-number worship;
- jump-test worship;
- proxy optimization replacing competitive 100m performance;
- completion of planned volume as proof of useful exposure;
- and calendar passage as proof of adaptation.

The exclusion of universal claims does not exclude conditional use of the
underlying training methods or organizational models.

## Evidence and Falsification Interface

Each claim should be linked to persistent Evidence Records using the
[Evidence Record Schema](../evidence/evidence_record_schema.md).

Evidence may Support, Challenge, Bound, or leave a claim Unresolved.

Live evidence and current Athlete Data enter as Hot Layer information.

They may change the current decision immediately when appropriate.

They do not automatically rewrite this Warm Layer framework or the Cold Layer.

A material challenge should produce a `REVIEW CANDIDATE` containing:

- claim identifier;
- current status;
- supporting and contradictory evidence;
- athlete-response pattern;
- affected conditions and decisions;
- proposed status or scope change;
- and uncertainty.

Repeated athlete response can strengthen or weaken contextual confidence.

It does not automatically establish a universal rule.

## Framework Selection Boundary

Use this framework as the Project Default only when it remains compatible with:

- the Constitution;
- the current Performance Problem;
- Athlete State;
- hard constraints;
- current evidence and applicability;
- expected marginal benefit;
- recovery and opportunity cost;
- transfer potential;
- competition timing;
- and uncertainty.

When another theory or organization has a stronger conditional claim, use that
alternative and record the reason.

When no candidate is sufficiently supported, use a bounded exploratory decision
or `Cannot Determine`.

The framework succeeds when it improves decision quality and competitive 100m
planning while remaining open to correction.
