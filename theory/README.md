# Theory Layer

Version: 0.1
Status: Draft
Date: 2026-09-15

## Purpose

The Theory layer contains candidate explanatory and organizational models that
can guide planning reasoning before all athlete-specific information is known.

It may include:

- explanatory models;
- organizational models;
- coaching heuristics;
- project priors;
- and explicit hypotheses about likely training relationships.

Theory answers:

> What candidate model or prior should reasoning begin with?

Theory has no constitutional authority.

The [Constitution](../CONSTITUTION.md) governs which forms of reasoning are
allowed.

If Theory conflicts with the Constitution, Theory must yield.

The initial project framework is
[100m Training Framework](./100m_training_framework.md).

## Theory Status

A Theory claim may carry more than one status when each label describes a
different dimension.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Project Default | Preferred starting prior for this project when relevant information does not justify another approach |
| Practice-Supported | Supported primarily by coherent coaching practice or project experience; not equivalent to strong comparative evidence |
| Evidence-Supported | Supported by applicable scientific or structured empirical evidence at a level stated in the claim record |
| Conditional | Valid only under stated athlete, target, timing, exposure, or constraint conditions |
| Exploratory | Used to generate information or test a bounded hypothesis |
| Project Hypothesis | Plausible project claim that lacks enough support for default or evidence-supported status |
| Contested | Material evidence or practice disagreement remains active |
| Unresolved | Available information does not support a stable direction |
| Deprecated | Retained for traceability but no longer recommended as a current prior |

`Project Default` does not mean universal law.

`Practice-Supported` does not mean scientifically established.

`Evidence-Supported` must retain population, protocol, outcome, external-
validity, and uncertainty limits.

Status belongs to a specific claim version, not to an entire tradition or
author by reputation.

## Theory Record Structure

A Theory claim should contain:

### Claim

The explanatory or organizational proposition being advanced.

### Rationale

The relationship that makes the claim plausible.

### Expected Planning Implication

How the claim may change attention, comparison, priority, organization, or
prescription when applicable.

### Applicable Conditions

The athlete, objective, state, time, exposure, and constraint conditions under
which the claim may be useful.

### Known Limitations

Boundaries, counterexamples, alternative explanations, and transfers that are
not established.

### Supporting Evidence

Evidence records or current project sources that strengthen the claim.

### Contradictory Evidence

Evidence records or observations that challenge or narrow the claim.

### Uncertainty

Unknowns that affect confidence or application.

### Revision or Falsification Trigger

Observations, athlete response, or stronger evidence that should weaken,
replace, narrow, or deprecate the claim.

### Project Status

One or more current status labels with version and review date.

## Theory Selection

Theory enters planning through:

Current Problem

→ Athlete State

→ Constraints

→ Candidate Theory Prior

→ Evidence and Applicability

→ Core-Model Interpretation

→ Rule-Governed Decision.

Theory must not automatically determine the Plan.

A default may be overridden when current Athlete State, constraints, evidence,
response, cost, transfer, or competition timing supports another option.

The planning record should identify which Theory claim influenced a decision
when that influence is material.

---

## Theory Conflict

When two theories imply different directions, preserve the conflict long enough
to identify the decision-relevant difference.

Compare them through:

- Constitution and Core-Model compatibility;
- current Performance Problem;
- Athlete State and Candidate Bottleneck;
- hard constraints;
- evidence quality and applicability;
- expected benefit, cost, risk, and transfer;
- reversibility;
- athlete-specific response;
- and uncertainty.

Do not select a theory through historical reputation, popularity, or a single
favorable outcome.

When no theory is clearly preferable, use a conditional choice, bounded test,
or `Cannot Determine` rather than false synthesis.

## Theory and Evidence

Theory provides prior structure.

Evidence updates confidence.

Athlete response updates confidence again.

Therefore:

Theory ≠ Truth.

Evidence ≠ Automatic Decision.

Observed Athlete Response ≠ Automatically Universal Evidence.

Supporting, contradictory, boundary-setting, and unresolved evidence must all
remain admissible.

The [Evidence Record Schema](../evidence/evidence_record_schema.md) defines the
minimum structure for persistent evidence.

## Knowledge Temperature Model

| Temperature | Contents | Update rate | Change authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold Layer | Constitution and Core Models | Very slow | Formal architecture review and explicit versioned revision |
| Warm Layer | Theory, Domain, and Rules | Deliberate and reviewable | Versioned update after conflict, evidence, and downstream effects are assessed |
| Hot Layer | Live Evidence, web retrieval, current Athlete Data, and task context | Rapid and task-specific | Immediate decision input within current authority and uncertainty limits |

Hot Layer information can change a current decision quickly.

It cannot directly rewrite the Cold Layer.

When repeated or high-value new evidence challenges a Core Model, create a
`REVIEW CANDIDATE` containing:

- the challenged claim;
- supporting and contradictory information;
- affected decisions and interfaces;
- confidence and applicability;
- and the proposed review scope.

Warm Layer updates should remain traceable and reversible when uncertainty is
material.

## Theory Update Policy

A Theory update should record:

- claim identifier and version;
- prior wording and status;
- proposed wording and status;
- evidence and athlete-response basis;
- contradictory evidence;
- affected Domain, Rules, and Workflows;
- revision or falsification trigger reached;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- and review date.

A repeatedly successful athlete-specific response can strengthen contextual
confidence without becoming a universal rule.

A failed outcome can weaken a claim only after exposure, measurement, recovery,
transfer, and competing explanations are considered.

Deprecated claims should remain discoverable when needed for traceability.
