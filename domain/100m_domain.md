# 100m Training Domain

Version: 0.1
Status: Draft
Date: 2026-09-15

## Purpose and Scope

This file defines a lightweight ontology and retrieval vocabulary for
competitive 100m training.

It identifies concept classes, canonical terms, common aliases, and search
language.

It does not reproduce the
[100m Performance Core Model](../core_models/02_100m_performance_model.md),
select training content, prescribe dose, or rank evidence.

Canonical terms improve interpretation and retrieval.

They do not establish that a concept is an independent capacity, a causal
bottleneck, or a conditionally appropriate intervention.

---

## 1. Sprint Performance Concepts

Race-phase terms are descriptive time-space structures.

They may overlap and transition continuously.

They are not automatically independent physiological or trainable capacities.

| Canonical term | Scope | Common aliases and retrieval terms |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Race initiation from the competition start process | sprint start; block start; starting performance; reaction and initiation |
| Block Clearance | Initial movement out of the blocks and first ground interactions | block exit; clearance; first-step mechanics; block clearance kinetics |
| Early Acceleration | Initial increase in velocity after clearance | initial acceleration; short acceleration; early race acceleration |
| Late Acceleration | Continued velocity development after the earliest steps | extended acceleration; mid-acceleration; late acceleration phase |
| Transition | Gradual change from acceleration-dominant organization toward upright high-velocity running | rise phase; acceleration-to-max-velocity transition; transition mechanics |
| Maximum Velocity | Highest velocity reached or a race segment centered on near-maximal velocity expression | MaxV; top speed; maximal velocity; peak velocity |
| Speed Maintenance | Preservation of velocity or limitation of decline after peak velocity | maximal velocity maintenance; late-race velocity maintenance; speed maintenance |
| Deceleration | Reduction in velocity across the later race | velocity loss; late-race deceleration; deceleration profile |
| Race Segment | A bounded race interval used to describe outcome or velocity development | split; segment; interval; race section |
| Velocity Profile | Velocity as a function of time or distance | race velocity curve; speed profile; velocity-distance profile |
| Acceleration Profile | Acceleration as a function of time or distance | acceleration curve; race acceleration profile |
| Competition Expression | Degree to which current capacity is expressed under race conditions | race-day expression; performance availability; competitive expression |
| Competitive 100m Performance | Terminal race outcome under defined competition and measurement conditions | 100m race performance; 100-m sprint performance; official 100m result |

Related terms must retain model level.

`Segment time`, `flying sprint time`, and `peak velocity` are not synonyms for
competitive 100m performance.

They may be component outcomes, indicators, or proxies depending on the
decision.

## 2. Sprint Intervention Families

An intervention family groups tasks by broad execution and retrieval context.

The family name does not uniquely define Training Stimulus.

Actual stimulus depends on task, dose, execution quality, Athlete State,
temporal placement, and environment as defined by the
[Stimulus-Dose-Cost Core Model](../core_models/03_stimulus_dose_cost_model.md).

| Canonical family | Common aliases | Typical English search terms | Broad intended-stimulus category |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acceleration Sprinting | acceleration runs; starts; short sprints | sprint acceleration training; acceleration sprint intervention; short sprint training | sprint-specific velocity development and force application during acceleration |
| Maximum-Velocity Sprinting | MaxV work; top-speed sprinting; fly work | maximum velocity sprint training; flying sprint; top speed exposure | high-velocity sprint expression and organization |
| Resisted Sprinting | sled sprint; towing; resisted acceleration | resisted sprint training; sled towing sprint; sprint resistance load | altered sprint force-velocity and task constraint under external resistance |
| Assisted Sprinting | overspeed; towing assistance | assisted sprint training; overspeed sprint; supramaximal velocity exposure | altered high-velocity exposure under assistance |
| Sprint-Float-Sprint Variants | ins-and-outs; change-of-pace sprints | sprint float sprint; ins and outs sprint; variable velocity sprint | transitions among sprint intensity or coordination conditions |
| Speed Endurance | speed-endurance running; sprint endurance | speed endurance training; sprint performance maintenance; repeated high-speed running | race-relevant high-velocity performance under accumulating cost |
| Special Endurance | long sprint endurance; special endurance I/II | special endurance sprint; long sprint training; sprint-specific endurance | longer sprint exposure with substantial metabolic and mechanical demand |
| Competition Exposure | race; meet; trial; round | 100m competition exposure; sprint race performance; competition rounds | terminal outcome, high-specificity exposure, stress event, and feedback source |
| Technical Sprint Drills | sprint drills; running drills; drill progressions | sprint technique drills; sprint coordination drill; sprint motor learning | constrained practice, coordination, or attentional emphasis |

These categories can overlap.

A resisted sprint remains sprinting, a competition can also provide maximum-
velocity or speed-endurance exposure, and a technical task can produce
meaningful mechanical or tissue stress.

Search and interpretation should record the implemented protocol rather than
relying on the family label.

## 3. Strength and Power Domain

Strength and power terms describe supporting capacities, outputs, contraction
emphases, or intervention families.

They are not synonyms for sprint performance.

| Canonical term | Scope | Common aliases and retrieval terms |
|---|---|---|
| Maximal Strength | Highest force capability under a defined task and condition | maximum strength; 1RM strength; maximal force capacity |
| Explosive Strength | Rapid force development under a defined task | explosive force; rate of force development; rapid force production |
| Power | Mechanical work rate or task-specific force-velocity output | mechanical power; peak power; mean power; power training |
| Ballistic Work | Tasks allowing continued acceleration through release, projection, or flight | ballistic training; loaded jump; throw training |
| Olympic-Lift Derivatives | Pulling, catching, or derivative tasks based on weightlifting movements | weightlifting derivatives; Olympic lifting variations; clean pull; jump shrug |
| Isometric Work | Force production with constrained or minimal joint displacement | isometric training; overcoming isometric; yielding isometric |
| Eccentric Work | Emphasis on force during muscle-tendon lengthening | eccentric training; eccentric overload; lengthening contraction |
| Unilateral Work | Task performed primarily with one limb or asymmetrical support | single-leg training; unilateral strength; split-stance work |
| Bilateral Work | Task performed with both limbs in a shared support pattern | double-leg training; bilateral strength; bilateral exercise |

`Power` must retain its measurement task and calculation method.

`Explosive` does not prove high sprint specificity.

`Unilateral` and `bilateral` describe task organization, not automatic transfer
or superiority.

## 4. Plyometric / SSC Domain

Plyometric and stretch-shortening-cycle terms describe task families and
mechanical organization.

They do not define a universal intensity hierarchy without dose, amplitude,
contact, surface, execution, and Athlete State.

| Canonical term | Scope | Common aliases and retrieval terms |
|---|---|---|
| Jump | Take-off and landing task using two or one limbs depending on protocol | vertical jump; broad jump; countermovement jump; jump training |
| Hop | Repeated or single take-off and landing on the same limb | single-leg hop; hopping; hop test; unilateral SSC |
| Bound | Alternating or repeated projection emphasizing horizontal or combined displacement | bounding; alternate bounds; speed bounds; horizontal plyometrics |
| Depth Jump | Drop from an elevation followed by an intended rapid rebound | depth jump; drop jump; rebound jump |
| Hurdle Jump | Jumping over one or more barriers | hurdle hops; hurdle jumps; repeated hurdle contacts |
| Repeated Contacts | Series of ground contacts treated as a dose dimension | contact volume; ground contacts; repeated jumps |
| Low-Amplitude SSC | Relatively small displacement or projection under the defined task | ankle hops; pogos; low-amplitude plyometrics |
| High-Amplitude SSC | Larger displacement, projection, or landing demand under the defined task | high-amplitude plyometrics; bounding; depth landing and rebound |

`Fast SSC` and `slow SSC` require an operational definition tied to the task and
measurement protocol.

Contact time alone does not fully define tissue cost or transfer.

## 5. Speed-Endurance Domain

Speed-endurance terminology varies across coaching traditions and research.

Term differences do not automatically establish separate physiological
entities.

| Canonical term | Typical usage | Aliases and retrieval terms | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed Endurance | Repeated or extended sprint exposure intended to preserve high performance as fatigue accumulates | sprint endurance; speed maintenance training; high-speed endurance | Distance, rest, intensity, and outcome must be extracted |
| Special Endurance | Longer sprint exposure with substantial race-relevant or metabolic demand | special endurance I; special endurance II; long sprint work | Usage varies by system; label alone is insufficient |
| Repeated-Sprint Ability | Ability to reproduce sprint efforts with incomplete recovery | RSA; repeated sprint performance; repeated sprint training | Often studied in team-sport populations; applicability to 100m planning must be assessed |
| Intensive Tempo | Submaximal or high-submaximal running with substantial density or metabolic demand | intensive tempo running; tempo endurance | Coaching definitions vary; do not infer intensity from the word `tempo` |
| Extensive Tempo | Lower-speed repeated running used for conditioning or technical rhythm in some systems | extensive tempo; tempo runs; recovery tempo | Low speed does not guarantee low total or tissue cost |
| Race Modeling | Exposure arranged to represent parts or relationships of race demand | race model training; split runs; broken sprint | May emphasize race structure without reproducing full competition |

Retrieval should include distance, intensity definition, rest, repetitions,
athlete population, and measured outcome.

Without those fields, comparison among labels is unreliable.

## 6. Technical Domain

Technique is the organization of movement used to solve a sprint task under
the athlete's current constraints.

Technique ≠ Single Ideal Movement Template.

| Canonical term | Scope | Common aliases and retrieval terms |
|---|---|---|
| Technical Organization | Coordination of force, timing, posture, segment motion, and task strategy | sprint mechanics; technical model; movement organization |
| Movement Solution | Athlete-specific way of satisfying task demands | coordination solution; motor solution; movement strategy |
| Task Constraint | Condition that changes the available movement solution | constraint-led task; environmental constraint; equipment constraint |
| Coordination | Temporal and spatial organization among actions and segments | intermuscular coordination; movement coordination; sprint coordination |
| Execution Stability | Ability to reproduce useful organization under relevant conditions | technical consistency; movement stability; repeatability |
| Technical Variability | Variation in movement across repetitions or conditions | movement variability; coordination variability |
| Coaching Cue | Instruction intended to alter attention or execution | external cue; internal cue; technical cue |
| Drill Transfer | Degree to which drill change appears in sprint execution | skill transfer; drill-to-sprint transfer; motor transfer |

No isolated foot path, posture, contact time, or joint angle defines universal
technical quality.

A technical observation must remain tied to task, phase, athlete, outcome,
measurement conditions, and uncertainty.

Improved drill execution does not automatically establish improved sprint
expression.

## 7. Monitoring and Testing Vocabulary

Monitoring terms describe observation sources.

They do not establish monitoring value or automatic decisions.

| Canonical term | Observed object | Common aliases and retrieval terms |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Elapsed time under a defined protocol | electronic timing; timing gates; stopwatch time; sprint timing |
| Split Time | Elapsed time over a defined race or sprint interval | segment time; interval time; 10m split; 30m split |
| Velocity | Speed estimated or measured over time or distance | sprint velocity; instantaneous velocity; average velocity; peak velocity |
| Jump Test | Output from a defined jump task | CMJ; squat jump; drop jump; RSI test |
| Strength Test | Output from a defined force or load task | 1RM; isometric force; dynamometry; maximal strength test |
| Bar Velocity | Velocity of an external load under a defined lifting protocol | velocity-based training; VBT; mean concentric velocity |
| Subjective Report | Athlete-provided description or rating | RPE; session RPE; wellness; readiness questionnaire |
| Tissue Report | Local symptom, soreness, discomfort, or tolerance information | pain report; tissue response; localized soreness |
| Video Observation | Visual or quantified information from recorded movement | sprint video analysis; kinematic observation; technical video |
| Competition Result | Official or verified race outcome and conditions | race time; wind reading; placing; round result |

Every measurement requires its protocol, context, comparison reference,
quality limits, and decision relevance.

Observed Data informs Athlete State.

It does not equal Athlete State.

## 8. Search Vocabulary

The following mappings support future English retrieval.

They are starting vocabularies, not exhaustive search strings.

| Concept or question | Useful aliases | Search-term components |
|---|---|---|
| 100m determinants | sprint determinants; race predictors; biomechanical factors | `100 m sprint performance determinants`; `trained sprinters`; `race analysis` |
| Start and clearance | block start; block exit; reaction and first steps | `sprint block clearance`; `start kinetics`; `100m start performance` |
| Acceleration | short sprint; early acceleration; extended acceleration | `sprint acceleration training`; `acceleration mechanics`; `trained sprinter intervention` |
| Maximum velocity | MaxV; top speed; flying sprint | `maximum velocity sprint training`; `flying sprint`; `peak sprint velocity` |
| Speed maintenance | deceleration; late-race performance; sprint endurance | `100m speed maintenance`; `sprint deceleration`; `speed endurance training` |
| Resisted sprint | sled sprint; towing; resisted acceleration | `resisted sprint training`; `sled load velocity decrement`; `trained sprinters` |
| Sprint technique | mechanics; coordination; kinematics | `sprint technique intervention`; `sprint coordination`; `biomechanics transfer` |
| Strength transfer | maximal strength; force capacity; resistance training | `strength training sprint performance transfer`; `trained sprinters`; `100m` |
| Power and ballistic work | loaded jump; weightlifting derivative; explosive strength | `power training sprint performance`; `ballistic training sprinters`; `weightlifting derivatives sprint` |
| Plyometric / SSC | jump; hop; bound; depth jump | `plyometric training sprint performance`; `stretch shortening cycle sprinters`; `jump transfer` |
| Dose response | volume; intensity; frequency; density; rest | `sprint training dose response`; `training volume`; `velocity loss`; `recovery` |
| Maintenance and detraining | retention; minimal dose; residual effect | `sprint maintenance dose`; `strength retention athletes`; `detraining sprinters` |
| Monitoring validity | reliability; measurement error; meaningful change | `sprint monitoring reliability`; `CMJ reliability athletes`; `timing gate validity` |
| Competition preparation | taper; peaking; freshness; performance expression | `sprint taper`; `100m competition preparation`; `training load race performance` |

Retrieval should add population, training status, sex, age, competitive level,
protocol, comparator, outcome, and date terms when they matter.

Negative or contrast terms can reduce confirmation bias, for example:

- adverse effects;
- no effect;
- null result;
- comparison;
- transfer failure;
- trained athletes;
- female sprinters;
- and long-term follow-up.

## Domain Interfaces and Update Boundary

[Domain Layer](./README.md) defines governance for these terms.

[Theory Layer](../theory/README.md) may use Domain terms to state candidate
models.

[Evidence Layer](../evidence/README.md) uses them to retrieve and index evidence.

Core Models determine the logical role of each retrieved object.

Rules determine whether an intervention or decision is justified.

Workflows determine execution order.

New terminology or repeated retrieval ambiguity may create a Domain
`REVIEW CANDIDATE`.

No new term automatically changes a performance model, theory status, or
planning decision.
